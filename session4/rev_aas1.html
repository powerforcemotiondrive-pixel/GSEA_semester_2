Session 4: Jobs in Aviation

Master the 18 essential technical and professional terms covering landside, airside, flight operations, air traffic control (ATCO), and civil aviation administration (TSEEAC / AFISO). Click any term to hear native pronunciation.

AFISO (Flight Info Officer)

01

A certified aeronautical station officer (often TSEEAC) providing traffic advisories, aerodrome conditions, and alerting services to aircraft at non-controlled aerodromes without separation authority.

Air Traffic Control Officer (ATCO)

02

A licensed professional legally authorized to issue mandatory control clearances and maintain safe tactical separation between aircraft in flight and maneuvering on active airport surfaces.

Turnaround Coordinator (TRC)

03

The ground handling operations lead on the apron responsible for orchestrating servicing teams, monitoring critical-path milestones, and ensuring punctual departure.

Ramp Agent

04

An airside ground handler responsible for loading and unloading baggage and cargo, positioning ground support equipment, and securing the aircraft servicing envelope.

Marshaller

05

A trained apron ground handler who uses illuminated wands or visual hand signals to guide an incoming aircraft along the taxi line to its exact parking mark.

Flight Operations Officer / Dispatcher

06

A licensed flight planning specialist who calculates fuel requirements, monitors en-route NOTAMs and weather, prepares flight plans, and co-signs the dispatch release with the captain.

Passenger Service Agent (PSA)

07

A landside airline or ground handling employee who assists travelers at check-in desks, checks travel documentation, reconciles hold luggage, and conducts gate boarding.

Aviation Safety Inspector

08

A civil aviation authority official (e.g. DGAC/DSAC, often TSEEAC) tasked with auditing airlines, maintenance repair organizations, and aerodromes for strict regulatory compliance.

Aerodrome Operations Coordinator

09

An airport duty specialist (often TSEEAC) responsible for inspecting runway pavement friction, monitoring obstacle limitation surfaces, managing wildlife hazards, and coordinating apron works.

Avionics Technician

10

A certified aircraft maintenance engineer specialized in troubleshooting, repairing, calibrating, and certifying cockpit flight management computers, radar, and navigation systems.

First Officer (Co-Pilot)

11

The second-in-command pilot on the flight deck who shares flight navigation, radio communications, and system monitoring duties with the Captain.

Purser / Cabin Manager

12

The senior cabin crew member responsible for supervising cabin attendants, directing in-flight passenger safety procedures, and coordinating with the flight deck.

Security Screening Officer

13

A certified landside security officer who operates X-ray scanners, millimeter-wave body scanners, and explosive trace detectors to screen passengers and carry-on luggage.

De-icing Rig Operator

14

A specialized ramp technician who operates aerial boom trucks to spray heated glycol mixtures onto aircraft surfaces to eradicate snow, frost, and ice contamination.

Pushback Tug Driver

15

A heavy-equipment ramp driver specialized in connecting towbars or towbarless cradles to safely push aircraft backward out of gate stands into the taxi lane.

Airfield Maintenance Technician

16

A technical specialist who inspects and maintains aeronautical ground lighting (AGL), taxiway guidance signage, runway markings, and perimeter security fences.

Refueller / Hydrant Operator

17

A ramp fueling technician who connects mobile fuel bowsers or underground hydrant dispensers to aircraft fuel valves to deliver precise, contamination-tested Jet-A1.

Load Controller (Weight & Balance)

18

An operations professional who calculates aircraft center-of-gravity (CG), models payload distribution, and generates the official load and trim sheet required for safe takeoff.
