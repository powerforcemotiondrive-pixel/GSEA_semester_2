Session 4: Biodiversity in Urban Areas and Airports

Review the 18 key terms for Biodiversity in Urban Areas and Airports to build your fluency in discussions, professional reading, and TOEIC practice.

Bird Strike Hazard

01

A collision between an airborne animal and an aircraft, posing acute mechanical danger during takeoff and landing.

Habitat Fragmentation

02

The division of expansive natural ecosystems into isolated parcels caused by highway and runway construction.

Urban Tree Canopy

03

The layer of leaves, branches, and stems formed by city trees, providing shade, cooling, and critical habitat for urban wildlife.

Ecological Corridor

04

A designated strip of vegetation linking wildlife populations separated by human infrastructure developments.

Bioacoustic Deterrent

05

Audio equipment broadcasting species-specific distress calls to disperse hazardous birds away from flight paths.

Wildlife Hazard Management (WHM)

06

A mandatory airport safety program systematically cataloging and mitigating animal attractants on airfields.

Biodiversity Net Gain (BNG)

07

A statutory planning requirement ensuring that infrastructure projects leave local natural habitats in a measurably better state.

Attractant Mitigation

08

Eliminating surface water bodies, refuse disposal sites, and roosting perches that attract hazardous wildlife to airfields.

Grassland Ecology

09

The study of short-grass prairie and meadow ecosystems adapted to frequent mowing and disturbed airfield soils.

Living / Green Roof

10

A building roof partially or completely covered with vegetation, creating urban habitats for pollinators and naturally insulating buildings.

Pollinator Sanctuary

11

Protected perimeter areas planted with native wildflowers to foster honeybee and butterfly biodiversity.

Urban Rewilding

12

An environmental conservation approach that restores natural ecological processes and native wilderness areas within cities and suburbs.

Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

13

A formal statutory study evaluating the prospective ecological consequences of proposed airport runway expansions.

Native Vegetation Re-seeding

14

Planting indigenous drought-tolerant grass varieties that require minimal mowing and provide unfavorable forage for geese.

Wetland Preservation

15

Protecting natural marshlands that filter surface runoff while engineering deterrents to prevent waterfowl concentration.

Ecosystem Services

16

The essential direct and indirect benefits that natural habitats provide to human society, including air filtration, stormwater management, and cooling.

Bio-Indicator Species

17

Organisms whose health, presence, or population shifts reflect the environmental quality of an entire airport watershed.

Non-Lethal Harassment

18

Trained border collies, pyrotechnics, and laser beams used to non-lethally displace birds from active runways.
