Grammar Point: Expressing Responsibilities, Duties & Authority

Master precise B2 functional language to describe professional obligations, team hierarchy, and regulatory jurisdictions across aviation careers.

Responsibility & Authority Formulas B2 Functional Focus
Grammar Patterns
BE RESPONSIBLE FOR + -ING • BE TASKED WITH + -ING • HAVE AUTHORITY OVER / TO • ENSURE THAT + CLAUSE
  • 1. BE RESPONSIBLE FOR + Gerund / Noun: Used to define primary recurring operational accountability ("The Turnaround Coordinator is responsible for supervising cargo loading").
  • 2. BE TASKED WITH + Gerund / Noun: Expresses an official operational mission or assigned duty ("AFIS officers are tasked with providing traffic information").
  • 3. HAVE AUTHORITY OVER / TO + Verb: Denotes legal command or jurisdictional power ("Tower ATCOs have authority over the active runway and authority to issue clearances").
  • 4. ENSURE THAT + Clause: Highlights verification and compliance auditing ("Safety inspectors ensure that airlines comply with DGAC regulations").
  • 5. REPORT TO vs. LIAISE WITH: Contrast vertical hierarchy ("The ramp agent reports to the coordinator") with horizontal coordination ("The dispatcher liaises with meteorologists").
⚠️ Common Preposition & False Friend Trap: Never say responsible of—always use responsible for + -ing. Also, strictly distinguish control (ATCO issuing binding separation orders) from provide information (AFISO advising pilots without executive control clearances).
Operational Responsibility Examples
✓ "The Turnaround Coordinator is responsible for ensuring that all ground support equipment is detached before pushback."
✓ "Unlike aerodrome controllers, AFISOs are tasked with providing traffic information rather than tactical separation clearances."
TOEIC Diagnostic Practice Part 5: Incomplete Sentences
Question 1 • Responsibility Prepositions Timed Pace: ~30s

The Turnaround Coordinator is directly responsible ________ supervising the loading of cargo and ensuring compliance with the weight schedule.

(A)with
(B)for
(C)of
(D)to
Click to Reveal Correct Answer & Analysis
CORRECT ANSWER: (B) for

"Responsible for" is the fixed prepositional collocation followed by a gerund ("supervising"). Avoid the common French false friend "responsible of".

Question 2 • Verb Patterns with Prepositions Timed Pace: ~30s

Unlike aerodrome controllers who issue clearances, AFIS officers are tasked with ________ flight information and meteorological updates to inbound pilots.

(A)provide
(B)provides
(C)providing
(D)provided
Click to Reveal Correct Answer & Analysis
CORRECT ANSWER: (C) providing

"Be tasked with" ends with a preposition ("with"), which must be followed by a gerund (-ing form: "providing").

Question 3 • Passive Voice in Documentation Timed Pace: ~30s

Before releasing the aircraft for departure, the flight dispatcher must verify that all load sheets ________ by the captain.

(A)have signed
(B)have been signed
(C)will sign
(D)signing
Click to Reveal Correct Answer & Analysis
CORRECT ANSWER: (B) have been signed

Passive voice is required because load sheets are the receiver of the action: they have been signed by the captain.

Question 4 • Conjunctions of Assurance Timed Pace: ~30s

The civil aviation safety inspector conducted an unannounced audit in order to ensure ________ ground handling personnel were strictly following apron safety protocols.

(A)that
(B)what
(C)which
(D)where
Click to Reveal Correct Answer & Analysis
CORRECT ANSWER: (A) that

"Ensure that" introduces a content clause detailing what condition is being secured or verified.

Question 5 • Prepositions of Concession Timed Pace: ~30s

The airport duty manager was commended for handling the ramp emergency calmly, ________ severe winter weather conditions.

(A)despite
(B)although
(C)unless
(D)whereas
Click to Reveal Correct Answer & Analysis
CORRECT ANSWER: (A) despite

"Despite" is a preposition of concession followed by a noun phrase ("severe winter weather conditions"). "Although" requires a full clause.
