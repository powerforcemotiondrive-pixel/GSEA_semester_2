How the Formative Evaluation Works

The Formative Evaluation takes place in pairs during a dedicated 30-minute tutorial slot. You and your partner will present your prepared task using visual supports, participate in a spontaneous Q&A discussion with your teacher, present evidence of your ongoing portfolio and TOEIC study, and discuss your personal strong and weak points.

The 30-Minute Oral Tutorial Roadmap

Stage 1 0–2 min

Welcome & Setup

Connect your laptop or set up your visual support. The teacher introduces the session and confirms your topic.

Get settled & relaxed
Stage 2 2–14 min

Pair Presentation

Deliver your prepared task with visual aids. Both students share talking time equally (~6 minutes per student). Do not read from notes!

Equal 50/50 speaking time
Stage 3 14–20 min

Teacher Q&A

Spontaneous interactive questions from the teacher to test your comprehension, justify your choices, and discuss alternatives.

Spontaneous dialogue
Stage 4 20–26 min

Portfolio & Self-Critique

Show evidence of vocabulary logs and TOEIC practice. Discuss your personal strong points and weak points (arrive prepared!).

Self-evaluation ready
Stage 5 26–30 min

Feedback & Targets

Receive concise, encouraging feedback from the teacher on your strengths and 1–2 practical action points for future sessions.

Mark out of 20 awarded

Module 1 Task: Choose Option A or Option B

Prepare with your partner before the evaluation
OPTION A Sustainable Travel

Design an Eco-Friendly Holiday Itinerary

Create a realistic 4-to-7-day sustainable travel itinerary for a family, group of students, or business travelers that actively minimizes carbon impact and respects local communities.

Key Requirements to Include:
  • Green Transportation: Low-carbon transit (high-speed rail, public transit, shared electric mobility; justify avoiding short domestic flights).
  • Eco-Certified Lodging: Hotels, eco-lodges, or homestays with verified sustainability certifications (waste management, renewable energy).
  • Responsible Activities: Nature conservation, community-based tours, local zero-kilometer dining, and cultural heritage protection.
  • DDRS Impact: Estimate how your itinerary reduces carbon footprint compared to standard mass tourism.
Target Lexis: carbon footprint, eco-certification, sustainable mobility, greenwashing
OPTION B Urban Tourism Policy

Compare Two Cities with Different Tourism Policies

Analyze and compare two popular international cities with contrasting approaches to managing tourism flows, preserving resident quality of life, and protecting historic heritage.

Key Requirements to Include:
  • Destination Contrast: Choose two distinct cities (e.g., Amsterdam vs. Copenhagen, Venice vs. Ljubljana, or Barcelona vs. Vienna).
  • Policy Analysis: Contrast visitor caps, tourist taxes/entry fees, short-term rental bans (Airbnb), cruise ship regulations, or zoning.
  • Resident & Environmental Impact: How do these measures affect local housing prices, noise, crowding, and the environment?
  • Your Evaluation: Which policy is more effective, sustainable, and fair? Propose 1–2 constructive recommendations.
Target Lexis: overtourism, visitor caps, congestion pricing, resident backlash, tourist levy

Track-Specific Linguistic Requirements (AG vs. AAS)

General English (AG Track) Focus:

Demonstrate strong command of complex grammar structures (e.g., conditionals: "If cities implemented tourist quotas, residents would...", passive voice, relative clauses, comparatives) and thematic lexis relating to sustainable travel and transport policies.

Technical English (AAS Track) Focus:

Demonstrate solid knowledge and active use of required aeronautical vocabulary (e.g., airport movement areas, passenger flow, apron safety, ground markings, runway numbering, FOD) and modal accuracy (obligation, permission, prohibition).

*Note: In both tracks, actively using terms from this website's vocabulary revision lists during your presentation and Q&A is required and evaluated.

Visual Supports: What to Prepare (and What to Avoid)

✅ DO:
  • Use 4 to 6 clean slides (PowerPoint, Canva, or PDF), a one-page infographic, or an itinerary map.
  • Include high-quality photos, route diagrams, comparison charts, and short bullet prompts.
  • Use visual aids as cues to speak directly to the teacher—make eye contact!
❌ DO NOT:
  • Do NOT write full paragraphs or complete sentences on your slides.
  • Do NOT read your presentation from paper, phone notes, or the screen.
  • Do NOT create more than 7 slides—you will run out of time during your 12-minute window.
💡

Top Tips to Excel in Your Formative Evaluation (B2 Friendly)

1. Respect the 50/50 Rule

Coordinate smoothly with your partner. Practice your handovers: "Now, [Partner] will present our lodging choices..." or "Building on that point..." Equal speaking time is part of the grade!

2. Plant Your Target Lexicon

Choose 4 to 6 specific vocabulary words from the session lists and write them on a reminder card. Speak them clearly and naturally in context during your presentation.

3. Signpost Your Talk

Guide the teacher through your presentation with clear linkers: To begin with, in contrast, furthermore, as a consequence, finally. This dramatically boosts your Fluency score.

4. Come Ready for Self-Critique

Don't wait for the teacher to point out flaws. Arrive ready to say: "My strong point is conversational fluency, but I am currently focusing on prepositions in timed TOEIC drills."

5. Handle Q&A Calmly

If you need a moment to think, use natural stalling phrases: "That's an interesting question; from our research..." rather than silence or switching to French.

6. Bring Your Portfolio Ready

Have your physical notebook or digital portfolio open and ready on your desk right from minute one. Show that you have tracked vocabulary and completed regular practice.
