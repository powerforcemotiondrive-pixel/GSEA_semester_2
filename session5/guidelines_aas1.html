How the Formative Evaluation Works

The Formative Evaluation takes place in pairs during a scheduled 30-minute tutorial slot. You and your partner will present a comprehensive technical profile of an airport of your choice, participate in spontaneous teacher Q&A, demonstrate active mastery of Module 1 technical vocabulary, present your personal vocabulary learning log, and conduct an autonomous self-critique of your personal strong and weak points.

Evaluation Activity: Technical Airport Profile & Operational Analysis

Pair Preparation Task

Task Overview

In pairs, select any commercial or civil aerodrome of your choice worldwide (e.g. Toulouse-Blagnac, London Heathrow, Chicago O'Hare, Madeira Funchal, St. Maarten Princess Juliana, Tokyo Haneda, Gibraltar, Courchevel). Over a 10-to-12 minute collaborative presentation, you must deliver a rigorous operational briefing analyzing the airport's layout, traffic constraints, safety hot spots, and incident history, while demonstrating that you have learned and mastered the specialized lexis from Sessions 1–4.

DIMENSION 1 Infrastructure

Technical Details & Physical Infrastructure

  • Runway System: Magnetic designators (azimuth headings), lengths, surface pavement (asphalt/concrete), friction characteristics, approach lighting systems (PAPI, ALS).
  • Airfield Movement Areas: Taxiway designators (Alpha, Bravo...), high-speed exit turnoffs, runway holding points (CAT I, CAT II/III stop bars), perimeter service roads.
  • Apron & Stands: Contact gates with passenger boarding bridges (jet bridges), remote stands, ground support equipment (GSE) staging lanes, hydrant fueling pipelines.
  • Air Navigation Facilities: Control tower height and line-of-sight, surface movement radar (A-SMGCS), ILS localizer and glideslope antennas.
DIMENSION 2 Traffic & Ops

Key Information, Traffic & Constraints

  • Operational Classification: International hub, secondary regional field, dedicated cargo hub, or mixed civil/military base.
  • Traffic Volumes: Annual passenger throughput, cargo tonnage, peak-hour movements, and primary based airlines.
  • Slot & Capacity Constraints: Coordinated slot allocation, turnaround time (TAT) targets, and Eurocontrol CTOT compliance.
  • Environmental Procedures: Noise abatement departure procedures (NADP), night curfews, acoustic buffer zones, and residential relations.
DIMENSION 3 Safety Hazards

Aerodrome Hot Spots & Operational Hazards

  • Published Hot Spots: Complex taxiway intersections, converging runway crossings, or blind spots from the control tower cab where runway incursion risk is elevated.
  • Apron Servicing Risks: Congested vehicle corridors, GSE pushback conflicts, and FOD hazards.
  • Adverse Weather Challenges: Critical crosswind runways, low visibility procedures (LVP / RVR minimums), de-icing pad management, and snow clearing sequences.
  • Wildlife & Obstacles: Bird hazard management programs and terrain obstacle limitation surfaces (OLS).
DIMENSION 4 Case Analysis

Notable Incidents, Accidents & Safety Lessons

  • Accident / Incident Case Study: Detail at least one historical or notable surface occurrence (e.g. runway incursion, landing overrun, apron collision, tailstrike).
  • Causal Chain: Analyze root causes (phraseology breakdown, human factors, loss of situational awareness, weather, or equipment failure).
  • Safety Upgrades Implemented: Explain physical or procedural changes introduced following the incident (e.g. illuminated red stop bars, radar safety logic, enhanced taxiway centerline markings, revised SOPs).

Mandatory Vocabulary Activation (Sessions 1 to 4)

To achieve top marks on Criterion 4 (Vocabulary & Aeronautical Lexis), your pair must naturally integrate at least 6 to 8 specialized technical terms learned during Module 1. Plant these terms into your speaking notes and deploy them accurately in context:

Session 1 (Aviation Survey): Passenger rights (EU261), slot allocation, security screening, noise abatement, hub carrier, suborbital trajectory.
Session 2 (Airfield Movement): Airside vs. landside, runway designators, taxiway markings, holding points, stop bars, magnetic heading, jet bridge.
Session 3 (Turnaround & GSE): Turnaround time (TAT), Ground Power Unit (GPU), pushback tug, belt loader, de-icing rig, Foreign Object Debris (FOD), chocks.
Session 4 (Jobs in Aviation): ATCO vs. AFISO, Turnaround Coordinator, Flight Dispatcher, Ramp Agent, responsible for, tasked with, authority over.

Visual Supports & Delivery Rules

✅ DO:
  • Use 4 to 6 clean, visual slides with photos, diagrams, and aerodrome charts.
  • Display the official airport layout map and zoom into published hot spots.
  • Use bullet cue cards only and maintain strong eye contact with the examiner.
❌ DO NOT:
  • Never write full paragraphs or long sentences on your slides.
  • Never read aloud from notes, prepared sheets, or screens (reading is severely penalized).
  • Never let one partner monopolize the speaking time.
Assessment Policy Note: TOEIC & Evaluation Rubric

In this oral evaluation, grading is criteria-referenced across fluency, pronunciation, grammatical accuracy, technical vocabulary deployment, teamwork, and your portfolio organization. TOEIC test performance does not factor into this oral evaluation score. TOEIC practice continues to be maintained and tracked independently on the platform.

💡 Examiner Success Tips
1. 50/50 Handover Rule: Rehearse smooth transitions. Each student must speak for approximately 6 minutes.
2. Show, Don't Read: Point to the aerodrome chart when explaining taxiway routing and hot spots.
3. Self-Critique Ready: Arrive with your vocabulary log open and 2 clear personal targets identified.
