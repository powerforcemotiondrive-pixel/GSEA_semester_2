How the Formative Evaluation Works

The Formative Evaluation takes place in pairs during a dedicated 30-minute tutorial slot. You and your partner will present your prepared task using visual supports, participate in a spontaneous Q&A discussion with your teacher, present evidence of your ongoing portfolio and TOEIC study, and discuss your personal strong and weak points.

The 30-Minute Oral Tutorial Roadmap

Stage 1 0–2 min

Welcome & Setup

Connect your laptop or set up your visual support. The teacher introduces the session and confirms your topic.

Get settled & relaxed
Stage 2 2–14 min

Pair Presentation

Deliver your prepared task with visual aids. Both students share talking time equally (~6 minutes per student). Do not read from notes!

Equal 50/50 speaking time
Stage 3 14–20 min

Teacher Q&A

Spontaneous interactive questions from the teacher to test your comprehension, justify your choices, and discuss alternatives.

Spontaneous dialogue
Stage 4 20–26 min

Portfolio & Self-Critique

Show evidence of vocabulary logs and TOEIC practice. Discuss your personal strong points and weak points (arrive prepared!).

Self-evaluation ready
Stage 5 26–30 min

Feedback & Targets

Receive concise, encouraging feedback from the teacher on your strengths and 1–2 practical action points for future sessions.

Mark out of 20 awarded

Module 2 Task: Choose Option A or Option B (Climate Resilience)

Prepare with your partner before the evaluation
OPTION A Aviation Infrastructure

Redesign an Airport for Climate Resilience

Select a major commercial airport facing pronounced climatic vulnerabilities (e.g., Nice LFMN, Amsterdam EHAM, Dubai OMDB, London Gatwick EGKK, Boston Logan KBOS, Singapore Changi WSSS) and design a comprehensive 2050 resilience masterplan.

Key Requirements to Include:
  • Hazard Profile: Evaluate acute threats (storm surges, flash floods, supercell microbursts) and chronic trends (sea-level rise, density altitude limits).
  • Physical Engineering: Permeable taxiway shoulders, subterranean retention reservoirs, sea dikes, and reflective cool pavement coatings.
  • Nature-Based Solutions: Constructed wetlands, bioswales, and airside wildlife hazard management (deterring birds from climb paths).
  • Contingency Operations: Heat mitigation for ramp crews, emergency flood diversions, and airport microgrid energy resilience.
Target Lexis: storm surge, density altitude, permeable pavement, bioswale, wildlife hazard management
OPTION B Urban Planning

Redesign a City / Urban District for Climate Resilience

Select an international metropolis facing severe urban heat islands or cloudburst inundation (e.g., Copenhagen, Rotterdam, Paris, Barcelona, Athens, Tokyo) and formulate an eco-district masterplan.

Key Requirements to Include:
  • Hazard Profile: Map urban heat islands (UHI), impermeable surface runoff rates, and historic precipitation return intervals.
  • Sponge City Interventions: Sunken water retention squares, permeable asphalt boulevards, and deep seawater/geothermal district cooling networks.
  • Urban Greening: 15-minute neighborhood zoning, pedestrian superblocks, rooftop green canopies, and biodiversity corridors.
  • Social Equity & Governance: Shaded public cooling islands for vulnerable residents, emergency heat warnings, and public capex defense.
Target Lexis: urban heat island, sponge city, 15-minute city, district cooling, biodiversity net gain
DIMENSION 1 Hazard Profile
Climate Hazard Assessment & Baseline Vulnerability
  • Acute Shocks: Flash floods, 100-year storm surges, supercell squalls, and microburst wind shear.
  • Chronic Trends: Rising mean summer temperatures, sea-level rise, and urban heat island amplification.
  • Critical Assets at Risk: Runways, underground power vaults, transit tunnels, and passenger terminals.
DIMENSION 2 Civil Engineering
Hard Engineering & Physical Infrastructure
  • Water Infiltration: Porous pavements, underground storm retention cisterns, and reinforced sea dikes.
  • Passive Cooling: High-albedo cool roofs, subterranean district cooling loops, and shaded walkways.
  • Energy Autonomy: Solar PV canopies, decentralized microgrids, and battery energy storage (BESS).
DIMENSION 3 Nature-Based Solutions
Nature-Based Solutions (NbS) & Ecological Co-Benefits
  • Natural Infiltration: Constructed wetlands, bioswales, rain gardens, and pocket micro-forests.
  • Biodiversity Net Gain (BNG): Restoring ecological corridors without attracting bird strike hazards near flight paths.
  • Social DDRS: Public park cooling corridors, equitable access, and green transit connectivity.
DIMENSION 4 Contingencies
Operational Contingency Protocols & Stakeholder Defense
  • Operating Protocols: Extreme heat night departure rescheduling, apron lightning closures, flood diversions.
  • Lifecycle Economics: Justifying initial capex against 30-year flood loss prevention and insurance savings.
  • Stakeholder Q&A: Defending choices against carrier pushback, resident skepticism, and regulatory barriers.
Mandatory Vocabulary Activation (Sessions 1 to 4)

To achieve full marks on Criterion 4, you and your partner must actively deploy at least 6 to 8 specialized technical terms from Module 2:

Session 1 (Extreme Weather): Heatwave, flash flood, storm surge, sea-level rise, atmospheric river, precipitation, resilience.
Session 2 (Sustainable Cities): 15-minute city, urban heat island, permeable pavement, low-emission zone, district cooling, bioswale.
Session 3 (Policy Arguments): Empirical evidence, concession, trade-off, prioritize investing in, advocate retrofitting, mandate.
Session 4 (Biodiversity): Biodiversity net gain, constructed wetland, ecological corridor, wildlife hazard, bird strike deterrent.

Track-Specific Linguistic Requirements (AG vs. AAS)

General English (AG Track) Focus:

Demonstrate command of Word Forms & suffixes, Subject-Verb agreement with collective nouns, Gerunds vs. Infinitives, polite concession and hedging ("While I acknowledge the upfront capex, empirical evidence indicates..."), and thematic resilience lexis.

Technical English (AAS Track) Focus:

Demonstrate command of flight physics & aerodynamics (lift, thrust, drag, weight, 3 axes, AoA, stalls), aircraft anatomy (flight control surfaces, structural components), commercial fleet metrics, and accident root-cause analysis.

*Note: In both tracks, actively using terms from this website's vocabulary revision lists during your presentation and Q&A is required and evaluated.

Visual Supports: What to Prepare (and What to Avoid)

✅ DO:
  • Use 4 to 6 clean slides (PowerPoint, Canva, or PDF), a one-page infographic, or an itinerary map.
  • Include high-quality photos, route diagrams, comparison charts, and short bullet prompts.
  • Use visual aids as cues to speak directly to the teacher—make eye contact!
❌ DO NOT:
  • Do NOT write full paragraphs or complete sentences on your slides.
  • Do NOT read your presentation from paper, phone notes, or the screen.
  • Do NOT create more than 7 slides—you will run out of time during your 12-minute window.
💡

Top Tips to Excel in Your Formative Evaluation (B2 Friendly)

1. Respect the 50/50 Rule

Coordinate smoothly with your partner. Practice your handovers: "Now, [Partner] will present our lodging choices..." or "Building on that point..." Equal speaking time is part of the grade!

2. Plant Your Target Lexicon

Choose 4 to 6 specific vocabulary words from the session lists and write them on a reminder card. Speak them clearly and naturally in context during your presentation.

3. Signpost Your Talk

Guide the teacher through your presentation with clear linkers: To begin with, in contrast, furthermore, as a consequence, finally. This dramatically boosts your Fluency score.

4. Come Ready for Self-Critique

Don't wait for the teacher to point out flaws. Arrive ready to say: "My strong point is conversational fluency, but I am currently focusing on prepositions in timed TOEIC drills."

5. Handle Q&A Calmly

If you need a moment to think, use natural stalling phrases: "That's an interesting question; from our research..." rather than silence or switching to French.

6. Bring Your Portfolio Ready

Have your physical notebook or digital portfolio open and ready on your desk right from minute one. Show that you have tracked vocabulary and completed regular practice.
