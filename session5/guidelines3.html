Formative Evaluation 3 (FE3) — Emerging Technology Presentation

The Formative Evaluation takes place in pairs during a dedicated 30-minute tutorial slot on Friday 27 February 2025 (Week 08) with teachers Geri MIDDLETON & Penny WRIGHT. You and your partner will present an in-depth technical profile and critical evaluation of an emerging aviation or non-aviation technology, defend system trade-offs during a spontaneous examiner Q&A, present evidence of your Vocabulary Log and TOEIC preparation drills, and evaluate your personal language development against official CEFR B2 standards.

Module 3 Task: Choose Option A or Option B (Emerging Technology)

Prepare with your partner before the evaluation
OPTION A Aviation Emerging Tech

Emerging Aviation Technology Pitch

Select a breakthrough aviation technology (e.g. Hydrogen fuel-cell aircraft, distributed electric propulsion / eVTOL urban air mobility, biometric single-token passenger gateways (IATA One ID), or AI-driven predictive fleet maintenance & digital twin systems) and present a comprehensive technical and commercial feasibility proposal.

Key Requirements to Address:
  • System Architecture: Explain the core mechanism (powertrain, sensor array, data pipeline, edge computing).
  • Operational & Decarbonization Impact: Quantify carbon abatement, turnaround acceleration, or route efficiency gains.
  • DDRS & Regulatory Compliance: Address battery recycling, hydrogen safety, cryogenic storage, or algorithmic data privacy (GDPR / ICAO).
  • Deployment Roadmap: Detail current TRL maturity, airport vertiport/grid retrofits, and projected 2030–2040 EIS (Entry Into Service).
Target Lexis: cryogenic storage, distributed propulsion, vertiport, predictive maintenance, digital twin, single-token biometric
OPTION B Non-Aviation Emerging Tech

Emerging Non-Aviation Technology Pitch

Select a cutting-edge cross-sector technological breakthrough (e.g. High-speed maglev transit corridors, smart grid AI optimization & renewable microgrids, decentralized autonomous identity & zero-knowledge verification, or autonomous public transport networks) and present an in-depth strategic analysis.

Key Requirements to Address:
  • Technical Mechanism: Core engineering principles, algorithmic models, software-hardware stack, and data processing.
  • Quantitative Societal Benefits: Energy grid balancing, passenger throughput, carbon displacement, or latency reduction.
  • DDRS & Ethical Safeguards: Mitigating algorithmic bias, explainable AI (XAI), digital surveillance vs. civil liberties, and EU AI Act compliance.
  • Scalability Horizon: Capital expenditure requirements, civil infrastructure overhaul, interoperability standards, and 10-year rollout schedule.
Target Lexis: algorithmic bias, explainable AI (XAI), black-box algorithm, data minimization, decentralized identity, grid balancing
DIMENSION 1 System Architecture
Technical Architecture & Core Mechanism
  • Core Engineering: Powertrain components, sensor arrays, neural network architectures, and hardware-software integration.
  • Data Flow: Telemetry ingestion, real-time edge processing, cloud pipelines, and fail-safe redundancy.
  • Grammar in Action: Deploy technical passive voice ("sensor telemetry is ingested at 50 Hz and routed to an edge computer...").
DIMENSION 2 Quantitative Gains
Quantitative Benefits & Operational Efficiency
  • Performance Metrics: Quantify specific efficiency improvements (e.g. 40% reduction in AOG downtime, 85% passenger boarding speedup, 0 g CO2/pax-km).
  • Comparative Analysis: Direct comparison against legacy baseline technologies using precise comparative metrics.
  • Grammar in Action: Future forms & predictive certainty ("this architecture is projected to cut operational expenditure by 28%...").
DIMENSION 3 Ethics & DDRS
DDRS, Ethical Dilemmas & Regulatory Standards
  • Environmental Footprint: Raw material extraction, battery lifecycle recycling, and data center compute electricity demands.
  • Societal & Ethical Safeguards: Mitigating algorithmic bias, Explainable AI (XAI), civil liberties, and EU AI Act compliance.
  • Grammar in Action: Cautious hedging & concession ("admittedly high initial capex may delay adoption; nonetheless, long-term ROI is compelling...").
DIMENSION 4 Scalability
Deployment Roadmap & Scalability Horizon
  • TRL Milestones: From lab prototype (TRL 4) to flight/service certification (TRL 8–9).
  • Infrastructure Overhaul: Charging vertiports, hydrogen bunkering networks, or grid substation upgrades.
  • Grammar in Action: Relative clauses & complex noun phrases ("vertiports which are co-located at multimodal transit hubs...").

Track-Specific Linguistic Requirements (AG vs. AAS)

General English (AG Track) Focus:

Demonstrate strong command of complex technical grammar (Active vs. Passive Voice in descriptions, Future Forms with Predictive Certainty, Cautious Hedging & Concession markers, Relative Clauses) and thematic lexis relating to artificial intelligence, clean propulsion, and digital identity.

Technical English (AAS Track) Focus:

Demonstrate solid knowledge and active use of aviation operational vocabulary (ATM sector coordination, runway safety, ground operations, emergency diversion procedures) and procedural phraseology.

*Note: In both tracks, actively using terms from this website's vocabulary revision lists during your presentation and Q&A is required and evaluated.

Visual Supports: What to Prepare (and What to Avoid)

✅ DO:
  • Use 4 to 6 clean slides (PowerPoint, Canva, or PDF), an architectural schematic, or a deployment timeline.
  • Include high-quality schematics, system block diagrams, efficiency graphs, and concise bullet cues.
  • Use visual aids as cues to speak directly to the teacher—maintain steady eye contact!
❌ DO NOT:
  • Do NOT write full paragraphs or complete sentences on your slides.
  • Do NOT read your presentation from paper, phone notes, or the screen.
  • Do NOT create more than 7 slides—you will run out of time during your 12-minute window.
💡

Top Tips to Excel in Your Formative Evaluation (B2 Friendly)

1. Respect the 50/50 Rule

Coordinate smoothly with your partner. Practice your handovers: "Now, [Partner] will break down the neural architecture..." or "Building on those operational metrics..." Equal speaking time is strictly assessed.

2. Plant Your Target Lexicon

Integrate 6 to 8 specialized vocabulary terms from Sessions 1–4. Speak them clearly and naturally in context during your pitch.

3. Signpost Your Talk

Guide the teacher through your presentation with clear linkers: To begin with, moving to architecture, in terms of DDRS safeguards, finally. This directly boosts your Fluency score.

4. Master Cautious Hedging

During Q&A, avoid rigid over-generalizations. Use hedging phrases: "The evidence suggests that...", "While implementation remains complex, the safety margins arguably justify..."

5. Handle Q&A Calmly

If you need a moment to think, use natural stalling phrases: "That's an interesting technical challenge; from our engineering analysis..." rather than silence or switching to French.

6. Bring Your Portfolio Ready

Have your physical notebook or digital portfolio open on your desk from minute one. Show that you have tracked vocabulary and completed regular practice.
