Target Competencies • B2 CEFR Level Formative Assessment Goals

What You Are Expected to Demonstrate

The Formative Evaluation measures both your collaborative spoken English (delivering a structured technical pitch and handling spontaneous Q&A) and your autonomous learning habits (actively recording target vocabulary, completing regular TOEIC drills, and critically evaluating your personal language development against official CEFR B2 standards).

5 Core Learning Objectives (B2 Standards)

01

Deliver a Structured Emerging Technology Presentation

Spoken Production

Present your prepared Emerging Technology Pitch (Option A: Aviation Tech or Option B: Non-Aviation Tech) clearly over 10–12 minutes without reading verbatim notes or slides. Systematically address the 4 core dimensions (Architecture, Quantitative Benefits, DDRS/Ethics, and Deployment Roadmap), connect your arguments with logical discourse markers (on the one hand, consequently, in addition, to summarize), and maintain a balanced 50/50 division of speaking time with your partner (~6 minutes each).

02

Engage in Spontaneous Technical Defense (Teacher Q&A)

Spoken Interaction

Respond spontaneously and accurately to follow-up questions from the examiner. Defend system trade-offs, explain regulatory compliance under GDPR or the EU AI Act, and comfortably build on or clarify your partner's points using concession and hedging strategies without switching languages.

03

Activate Specialized Vocabulary from the Platform Lists

Lexical Range

Naturally integrate at least 6 to 8 specialized technical terms from Sessions 1–4 (e.g. predictive maintenance, digital twin, cryogenic storage, distributed propulsion, vertiport, algorithmic bias, explainable AI, biometric verification, decentralized identity). Pronounce terms with correct syllable stress and avoid vague colloquial fillers.

04

Demonstrate Grammatical Precision & Syntactic Range (Track-Specific)

Grammatical Control

General English (AG): Show confident use of technical Passive Voice (data is ingested, algorithms are trained), Future Forms with Predictive Certainty (is bound to, will inevitably), Cautious Hedging & Concession (admittedly... nonetheless), and Defining/Non-defining Relative Clauses.
Technical English (AT): Show high accuracy with modal verbs of obligation, permission, and prohibition and standard ATM phraseology.

05

Exhibit Autonomous Portfolio Habits & Constructive Self-Critique

Autonomous Learning

Present an organized portfolio with recorded session terms, personal reflections, and evidence of regular TOEIC practice drills. Arrive ready to discuss your personal strong points and weak points honestly, identifying 2 clear action targets for Semester 2.

🎯 Linguistic Focus Comparison by Program

General English (AG Track) Communication & Debate
  • Technical Passive Voice: Describing autonomous workflows ("sensor data is ingested and processed in real time").
  • Predictive Certainty: Using future forms with precision ("this is bound to accelerate adoption by 2035").
  • Cautious Hedging: Nuanced argumentation during defense ("admittedly high initial capex poses hurdles; nonetheless...").
  • Complex Noun Phrases: Defining and non-defining relative clauses for technical definitions.
Technical English (AAS Track) Aviation Operations
  • Aeronautical Modals: Strict distinction between obligation (must), prohibition (are prohibited from), and recommendation (should).
  • Operational Lexis: Precise use of ATM, airfield geometry, taxiway markings, and ground operations terminology.
  • Radiotelephony Standard: Clean, unambiguous articulation avoiding conversational filler words during technical briefings.
