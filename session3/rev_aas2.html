Session 3: Commercial Aircraft & Airlines

Master the 18 key vocabulary terms for Commercial Aircraft and Airlines to evaluate fleet planning, aircraft classifications, manufacturer duopolies, and airline business models with technical precision and spoken fluency. Click any term to hear its pronunciation.

Narrowbody

01

A single-aisle commercial passenger aircraft typically seating 100 to 240 passengers with 6 seats abreast.

Widebody

02

A twin-aisle commercial airliner seating 250 to 500+ passengers with 7 to 10 seats abreast used for long-haul routes.

Regional Jet

03

A short-to-medium-range jet airliner typically seating 50 to 120 passengers on feeder routes.

Turboprop

04

An aircraft powered by a gas turbine engine geared to a propeller, highly efficient at low altitudes and short distances.

Freighter

05

A dedicated cargo aircraft with main-deck loading doors designed exclusively to carry freight containers.

Range

06

The maximum distance an aircraft can fly between takeoff and landing without refueling.

Payload

07

The total revenue-generating load carrying capacity of an aircraft, including passengers, luggage, and commercial cargo.

Maximum Takeoff Weight (MTOW)

08

The maximum certified total weight at which an aircraft is legally permitted to attempt takeoff.

Fuel Burn

09

The rate of fuel consumed by an aircraft engine during flight, measured in kilograms or liters per hour.

Manufacturer

10

An aerospace corporation, such as Airbus or Boeing, that designs, certifies, and builds aircraft.

Duopoly

11

A market condition where two major suppliers dominate sales, notably Airbus and Boeing in large commercial jetliners.

Flag Carrier

12

A legacy national airline registered in a specific country, often historically state-owned (e.g. Air France, British Airways).

Low-Cost Carrier (LCC)

13

An airline operating with a lean cost structure, high fleet utilization, point-to-point routes, and unbundled fares (e.g. Ryanair, easyJet).

Hub-and-Spoke

14

A route network model where flights connect through a central hub airport to transfer passengers onto connecting flights.

Point-to-Point

15

A route network model providing direct nonstop flights between origin and destination without transiting a central hub.

Fleet Renewal

16

The phased retirement of aging aircraft and replacement with newer, quieter, and more fuel-efficient models.

Cabin Configuration

17

The physical seating arrangement inside an airliner, divided into Economy, Premium, Business, and First Class.

Turnaround Efficiency

18

The capability of an airline and ground crew to deplane, service, refuel, and reboard an aircraft in minimum gate time.
