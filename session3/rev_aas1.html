Session 3: Ground Operations & Turnaround Management

Master the 18 essential technical terms covering apron ground servicing equipment (GSE), turnaround milestones, ramp safety protocols, and operational coordination. Click any term to hear native pronunciation.

Wheel Chocks

01

Heavy rubber or wooden blocks wedged in front of and behind landing gear tires to immobilize aircraft on the apron stand.

Pushback Tug

02

A heavy low-profile tractor equipped with a towbar or wheel clamp designed to push airliners backward out of gate stands.

Ground Power Unit (GPU)

03

A mobile diesel generator or electrical bridge cable providing 115V AC 400Hz power to the aircraft while main engines are off.

Belt Loader

04

A motorized inclined conveyor vehicle used for conveying loose baggage, mail sacks, and small cargo into lower aircraft holds.

Marshaller

05

A trained apron agent who directs flight crew using illuminated orange wands or hand signals to guide the aircraft onto the stop line.

Turnaround Time (TAT)

06

The scheduled window between block-on (chocks set) and block-off (pushback) during which all servicing must finish.

Apron (Ramp)

07

The designated airfield pavement zone where aircraft park to disembark travelers, refuel, load cargo, and receive maintenance.

Foreign Object Debris (FOD)

08

Loose items (bolts, luggage tags, tools, rocks) found on apron stands capable of being ingested into jet engines.

Jet Bridge (Airbridge)

09

An enclosed, weatherproof telescopic walkway extending from an airport terminal departure lounge directly to the cabin entrance.

Hydrant Dispenser (Fueler)

10

A specialized vehicle that connects underground apron fuel pits to the aircraft wing valves to pump Jet A-1 under pressure.

Catering Truck (Hi-Lift)

11

A box truck mounted on a heavy scissor-lift mechanism that elevates to galley doors to exchange meal carts.

De-icing Rig

12

A boom-equipped tanker truck spraying heated glycol fluid over aircraft wings and fuselage to remove ice and frost.

Equipment Restraint Area (ERA)

13

The painted boundary around an aircraft parking stand inside which vehicles may not enter until the aircraft stops and chocks are in.

Load Sheet & Trim

14

The official document certifying aircraft center of gravity (CG), total gross weight, cargo distribution, and fuel quantities before departure.

Baggage Tug & Carts

15

Small, nimble tractor vehicles towing a train of open or enclosed wheeled dollies carrying passenger luggage between sorting and stands.

Ramp Coordinator (TRC)

16

The ground handling supervisor directly managing simultaneous turnaround activities, fueling timing, and crew briefing on the stand.

Knock-on Delay (Reactionary)

17

A secondary delay propagating to subsequent flights due to the late arrival of the inbound aircraft or crew duty time limits.

All-Clear Signal

18

The formal safety confirmation by the ground marshaller showing the bypass steering pin held aloft indicating vehicles have cleared.
