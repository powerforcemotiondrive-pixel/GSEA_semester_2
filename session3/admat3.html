Grammar Point: Conjunctions & Transition Words & TOEIC Practice

Learn how to choose between conjunctions (which need a full sentence) and prepositions (which need only a noun) in TOEIC Part 5.

Connecting Sentences & Ideas B1–B2 Grammar Focus
Connecting Words
Conjunction: [Although / Because] + Subject + Verb | Preposition: [Despite / Due to] + Noun

Core Rules: Look at what comes after the blank to pick the right connecting word:

  • 1. Conjunctions (Although, Even though, Because, Since): Followed by a complete sentence with a Subject and a Verb ("Although the model worked...").
  • 2. Prepositions (Despite, In spite of, Because of, Due to): Followed only by a noun or an -ing word, never a full subject + verb ("Despite high accuracy...").
  • 3. Transition Words (However, Therefore, Furthermore): Start a new sentence after a period or semicolon, usually followed by a comma ("However, regulators hesitated.").
⚠️ TOEIC Trap Alert: The "Although vs. Despite" trap! If you see a Subject and a Verb after the blank, choose Although / Even though. If you see only a noun phrase, choose Despite / In spite of!
Governance & Ethics Examples
✓ "Although the model demonstrated accuracy, bias audits remained mandatory." (+ Clause)
✓ "Despite high statistical accuracy, public deployment was delayed." (+ Noun Phrase)
TOEIC Diagnostic Practice Part 5: Incomplete Sentences
Sample Item • Reading Section Timed Pace: ~30s

________ the algorithmic predictive policing system demonstrated high statistical precision in pilot tests, municipal regulators suspended its deployment due to civil liberty concerns.

(A) Despite
(B) Although
(C) However
(D) In spite of
💡 Click to Reveal Answer & TOEIC Strategy Analysis ▼
Correct Answer: (B) Although

Why (B) is correct: What follows the blank is a complete sentence with its own Subject ("the algorithmic predictive policing system") and Verb ("demonstrated"). Because we have a full Subject + Verb, we must use the conjunction Although, not a preposition like Despite.

Why the Other Choices Are Incorrect:
  • (A) Despite: Preposition; can only be followed by a noun, not a full sentence with Subject + Verb.
  • (C) However: Transition word; connects two separate sentences (usually after a period or semicolon), cannot link two clauses with just a comma.
  • (D) In spite of: Preposition phrase; same rule as Despite (needs a noun, not Subject + Verb).
📌 Quick Test Tip: Always check if there is a verb after the blank. If there is a Subject + Verb $\rightarrow$ use Although / Because. If there is only a noun $\rightarrow$ use Despite / Because of.
