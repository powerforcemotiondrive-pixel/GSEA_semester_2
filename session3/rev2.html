Session 3: Building and Supporting Arguments

Review the 18 key terms for Building and Supporting Arguments to build your fluency in discussions, professional reading, and TOEIC practice.

Argument Structure (Toulmin)

01

A practical method to build strong arguments by linking your main idea (claim) with facts (evidence) and logical explanation (reasoning).

Cautious Language (Hedging)

02

Careful words and phrases (it seems, appears to be, arguably) used to soften statements and avoid sounding 100% certain.

Concession Phrases

03

Words that accept a valid point from the other side before presenting your own argument (although, while it is true that, despite).

Ad Hominem Fallacy

04

An unfair debate tactic that attacks a person's character instead of responding to their argument.

Straw Man Fallacy

05

Exaggerating or distorting an opponent's argument to make it easy to attack and defeat.

Empirical Evidence

06

Factual proof and data gathered through research, experiments, or real-world measurements to support an argument.

Carbon Border Tax (CBAM)

07

A tariff on carbon-intensive imports designed to prevent carbon leakage and level the playing field for clean domestic industry.

Required Transport Shift

08

Government rules that require passengers or freight to use rail rather than planes or trucks on routes with good train service.

Precautionary Principle

09

The doctrine that preventative action should be taken in the face of scientific uncertainty regarding environmental harm.

Strong Counter-Argument

10

A counter-argument backed by solid facts that disproves an opposing point of view.

Counter-Argument

11

An opposing viewpoint or objection presented to challenge, question, or disprove an original argumentative claim.

Logical Fallacy

12

A flaw or error in reasoning that undermines the validity and soundness of an argumentative structure.

Slippery Slope

13

An unfounded assertion that an initial action will inevitably lead to an extreme, disastrous chain of consequences.

Burden of Proof

14

The procedural obligation of the party proposing a controversial claim or policy change to substantiate it with evidence.

Oxford Debate Format

15

A structured competitive debate protocol where teams argue for or against a specific motion under strict time limits.

Persuasive Language

16

Words, rhetorical phrasing, and stylistic devices chosen intentionally to influence, convince, and engage an audience.

Nuanced Qualifier

17

Terms (predominantly, under specific constraints, in most verified instances) establishing limits on a claim's universality.

Peer-Reviewed Consensus

18

The collective judgment of international scientific experts verified through rigorous independent methodological review.
