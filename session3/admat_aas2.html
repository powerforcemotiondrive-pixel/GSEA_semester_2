Grammar Point: Comparative, Superlative & Ratio Structures in Fleet Economics

Review core aerodynamic language structures and complete the diagnostic Part 5 exam question.

Aerospace Language Clinic B1–B2 Grammar Focus
Fleet Evaluation Formula
[Aircraft A] + is considerably more/less + Adj + than [Aircraft B] || Twice as + Adj + as

Key Communicative Guidelines:

  • 1. Modified Comparatives for Operational Metrics: Accentuates fleet performance differences ('The A321neo is significantly more fuel-efficient per seat-kilometer than older generation twinjets').
  • 2. Superlatives for Market Extremes: Identifies category leaders ('The Boeing 777X boasts the widest commercial passenger cabin and longest range among new twin-aisle jets').
  • 3. Expressing Multipliers & Ratios: Compares financial and cargo yields ('Widebody freighters carry nearly three times as much cargo payload as regional turboprops').
  • 4. Proportional Statements (The more... the more...): Links variables ('The lighter the composite fuselage structure, the lower the fuel burn over long-haul sectors').
⚠️ TOEIC Part 5 Comparative Modifiers Alert: Never use 'very' or 'more' with comparative adjectives (incorrect: 'very cheaper'). Always use degree modifiers: 'significantly', 'considerably', 'substantially', or 'slightly' (correct: 'significantly cheaper').
Authentic Operational Examples
✓ Low-cost carriers achieve considerably higher daily aircraft utilization than legacy network carriers.
✓ The new turbofan variant delivers a 15% reduction in carbon emissions compared to its predecessor.
