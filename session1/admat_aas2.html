Grammar Point: Cause-and-Effect Structures & Conditional Relationships in Flight Physics

Review core aerodynamic language structures and complete the diagnostic Part 5 exam question.

Aerospace Language Clinic B1–B2 Grammar Focus
Aero Cause & Effect Formula
Due to / As a result of + Noun Phrase, [Consequence] || If + Present, [Result]

Key Communicative Guidelines:

  • 1. Cause & Prepositional Linkers (Due to / Owing to / Because of): Introduces aerodynamic forces triggering an outcome ('Due to increased camber, airflow accelerates over the upper surface').
  • 2. Result Linkers (Consequently / As a result / Leading to): Links physical actions to reactions ('Air pressure decreases above the wing, consequently generating lift').
  • 3. Zero Conditional (Scientific Invariants): Expresses universal physical laws ('If the angle of attack exceeds 15 degrees, the airflow separates and the wing stalls').
  • 4. First Conditional (Operational Contingencies): Expresses flight deck actions ('If airspeed drops below Vref during approach, the flight crew will advance the throttles immediately').
⚠️ TOEIC Part 5 Cause Linker Alert: 'Due to' and 'because of' MUST be followed by a noun or noun phrase. 'Because' and 'since' are conjunctions followed by a subject + verb clause.
Authentic Operational Examples
✓ Due to boundary layer separation at high angles of attack, the aircraft experiences severe aerodynamic buffeting.
✓ If the pilot increases pitch attitude without adding thrust, airspeed will decay rapidly.
