Grammar Point: The Present Perfect Tense & TOEIC Practice

Learn how the Present Perfect connects past actions to the present moment, and master key time clues in TOEIC Part 5.

How to Use the Present Perfect B1–B2 Grammar Focus
Sentence Pattern
Subject + have / has + Past Participle (V3)

When to Use It: Use the Present Perfect when an action that happened in the past is connected to the present:

  • 1. Actions continuing now: Started in the past and continue into the present, signaled by since (+ starting point, e.g., since 2021) or for (+ duration, e.g., for three years).
  • 2. Past action with a present result: The exact past time is not mentioned; what matters is the result right now (*"We have updated our safety procedures"*).
  • 3. Recent updates: Marked by signal words like already, yet, recently, just, or lately.
⚠️ The #1 Past vs. Present Perfect Trap: Never use have / has + V3 with finished past dates or times (like yesterday, in 2020, two hours ago, or last year). When you see a specific completed time, always choose the Past Simple (*introduced*, *allocated*)!
Workplace & Transit Examples
✓ "The European Commission has allocated new funding for high-speed rail corridors since 2021."
✓ "Airport ground staff have not finalized the passenger rebooking procedures yet."
TOEIC Diagnostic Practice Part 5: Incomplete Sentences
Sample Item • Reading Section Timed Pace: ~30s

The European Transport Commission ________ several new high-speed rail corridors across member states over the past three years.

(A) introduces
(B) was introduced
(C) has introduced
(D) is introducing
💡 Click to Reveal Answer & TOEIC Strategy Analysis ▼
Correct Answer: (C) has introduced

Grammar Analysis: The phrase "over the past three years" is a major clue: it describes a time period starting in the past and continuing up to right now. An action that stretches into the present requires the Present Perfect: have / has + past participle (has introduced).

Why the Other Choices Are Incorrect:
  • (A) introduces: Present simple; used for daily routines or general facts, not a continuous trend over three years.
  • (B) was introduced: Passive past simple; would mean the Commission itself was introduced, which makes no sense.
  • (D) is introducing: Present continuous; describes an action happening right now this minute, not a three-year record.
📌 TOEIC Strategy Rule: Time markers such as over the past/last [period], since [date], lately, and so far are primary clues in TOEIC Part 5 that signal the need for have / has + past participle.
