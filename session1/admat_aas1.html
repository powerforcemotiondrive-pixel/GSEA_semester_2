Grammar Point: Modal Verbs of Obligation, Permission & Prohibition

Master the official modal and semi-modal structures governing airline security, cockpit defense protocols, and TOEIC Part 5 regulatory questions.

Modals in Aviation Safety & Operations B1–B2 Grammar Focus
Regulatory Formula
Subject + Modal / Semi-Modal + Base Verb (Infinitive)

The 4 Essential Regulatory Functions:

  • 1. Strict Obligation (Must / Have to / Be required to): Mandatory safety rules (*"All commercial aircrew must carry government credentials"* / *"Passengers are required to follow crew instructions"*).
  • 2. Strict Prohibition (Must not / May not / Be prohibited from): Unlawful actions (*"Travelers are strictly prohibited from entering the flight deck without authorization"*).
  • 3. Official Permission (May / Can / Be authorized to): Legal authorization (*"Deputized Federal Flight Deck Officers are authorized to carry defensive equipment in flight"*).
  • 4. Operational Recommendations (Should / Be advised to): Best practice guidelines (*"Carriers should notify passengers of schedule revisions in advance"*).
⚠️ TOEIC Part 5 Verb Pattern Alert: Pure modals (must, may, should, can) are always followed directly by the bare infinitive (no "to"). However, regulatory semi-modals require to + verb (is required to submit, is authorized to board) or preposition + gerund (prohibited from smoking).

TOEIC Diagnostic Practice Part 5: Incomplete Sentences
Sample Item • Reading Section Timed Pace: ~30s

Under revised flight deck defense protocols, certified airline captains are officially authorized ________ defensive equipment exclusively inside the cockpit.

(A) carry
(B) to carry
(C) carrying
(D) carried
Click to Reveal Correct Answer & Explanations
CORRECT ANSWER (B) to carry

Grammar Analysis: The predicate uses the passive semi-modal construction "be authorized + to-infinitive". In TOEIC grammar, expressions of official permission or obligation such as be permitted to, be allowed to, and be authorized to require a to + base verb complement.

Why the Other Choices Are Incorrect:
  • (A) carry: Bare infinitive; only used after pure modals (e.g., may carry, must carry), not after passive adjectives like authorized.
  • (C) carrying: Gerund; incorrect following authorized (used after prepositions, e.g., prohibited from carrying).
  • (D) carried: Past participle; results in an ungrammatical double participle construction.
📌 TOEIC Strategy Rule: Memorize the three key regulatory verb patterns: (1) modal + bare verb, (2) be required/authorized + to + verb, and (3) be prohibited from + -ing.
