Session 1: Extreme Weather

Review the 18 key terms for Extreme Weather to build your fluency in discussions, professional reading, and TOEIC practice.

Heatwave

01

An extended period of abnormally high atmospheric temperatures, often accompanied by high humidity, which stresses public health, strains power grids, and softens transport surfaces.

Wildfire / Forest Fire

02

An uncontrolled inferno spreading rapidly through timberland and dry vegetation, exacerbated by heat and drought, generating toxic smoke plumes and regional transport closures.

Severe Drought

03

An extended deficit in regional precipitation leading to acute water scarcity, agricultural failure, reduced hydroelectric capacity, and parched, fire-prone soils.

Desertification

04

The progressive degradation of fertile land into arid desert, driven by rising global temperatures, recurring droughts, deforestation, and unsustainable land use.

Flash Flood

05

A sudden, violent surge of floodwater triggered by torrential rainfall within a brief window, overwhelming drainage infrastructure and submerging transit corridors.

Storm Surge

06

An abnormal, destructive rise in sea level propelled ashore by low-pressure cyclones or hurricane winds, inundating coastal settlements and low-lying airports.

Sea-Level Rise

07

The ongoing global increase in ocean elevation driven by thermal expansion of warming seawater and the accelerated melting of glaciers and ice sheets.

Atmospheric River

08

A long, narrow corridor of concentrated water vapor in the atmosphere that channels tropical moisture, triggering historic deluge and widespread flooding upon landfall.

Supercell Thunderstorm

09

An organized, severe rotating storm system that produces destructive hail, tornadoes, and torrential rains, causing severe damage to infrastructure.

Polar Vortex Disruption

10

A destabilization of high-altitude Arctic winds that allows freezing polar air masses to plunge southward, causing severe and paralyzing cold snaps.

Early Warning System

11

Advanced meteorological networks and public communication alerts that notify communities of impending severe storms, floods, or extreme weather before disaster strikes.

Flood Defenses / Barriers

12

Engineered structures such as sea walls, levees, and storm surge gates designed to protect cities and transport corridors from rising floodwaters.

Greenhouse Effect

13

The planetary warming process where heat-trapping atmospheric gases (CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide) absorb outgoing infrared radiation instead of letting it escape to space.

Climate Tipping Point

14

A critical environmental threshold beyond which a minor change triggers self-reinforcing, irreversible shifts in Earth systems (e.g., permafrost thaw, ice-sheet collapse).

Ocean Acidification

15

The ongoing decline in ocean pH caused by absorbing excess carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, disrupting marine food chains and coral reefs.

Glacial Retreat

16

The accelerating worldwide loss of alpine glaciers and polar ice shelves due to sustained global warming, diminishing vital freshwater reservoirs.

Climate Resilience

17

The capacity of socio-economic systems, supply chains, and civil infrastructure to anticipate, absorb, recover from, and adapt to hazardous climate-related shocks.

Decarbonization

18

The systematic elimination or progressive reduction of greenhouse gas emissions across aviation, transport, industry, and energy sectors to achieve net-zero targets.
