Session 1: Artificial Intelligence

Review the 18 key terms for Artificial Intelligence to build your fluency in discussions, professional reading, and TOEIC practice.

Predictive Maintenance

01

Using sensor telemetry and machine learning models to forecast equipment failures before costly breakdowns occur in industrial and transport systems.

Machine Learning (ML)

02

Algorithms that train statistical models on historical data to identify patterns and make predictions without explicit rules.

Neural Network

03

A computational model inspired by biological brains, composed of layered interconnected nodes that process complex data.

Digital Twin

04

A virtual real-time digital replica of a physical machine, building, or transport system used to simulate stress, wear, and performance.

Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)

05

A system architecture where an automated algorithm requires human operator validation before executing critical actions.

Algorithmic Bias

06

Systematic and unfair discriminatory outcomes in automated decisions caused by skewed training datasets.

Computer Vision

07

AI technology enabling computers and automated software to analyze, interpret, and extract meaningful data from digital images and real-time video feeds.

Generative AI

08

Artificial intelligence systems (such as large language models and image generators) capable of creating original text, images, code, or media from human prompts.

Natural Language Processing (NLP)

09

Algorithms enabling computers to parse, understand, interpret, and generate human speech and written language across software applications.

Compute Carbon Footprint

10

The substantial electrical energy and greenhouse emissions produced by massive AI model training data centers.

Explainable AI (XAI)

11

Machine learning models designed so that human engineers can clearly understand and audit how decisions were reached.

Workplace Automation

12

The application of AI algorithms, software bots, and robotic systems to perform repetitive professional and administrative tasks previously handled by humans.

Deterministic Algorithm

13

A computational process that produces the exact same output every time given a specific set of input parameters.

AI Hallucination

14

An occurrence where a generative AI model creates incorrect, fabricated, or nonsensical information while presenting it with high grammatical confidence.

Synthetic Training Data

15

Artificially generated computer datasets used to train machine learning models on complex, rare, or safety-critical scenarios without real-world risk.

Black Box Algorithm

16

A deep neural network whose internal decision weights cannot be directly inspected or explained by human operators.

Cyber-Physical Security

17

Protecting integrated AI software and physical avionics hardware from malicious interference or spoofing.

Ethical AI Guidelines

18

Formal regulatory standards, transparency rules, and corporate governance frameworks designed to ensure artificial intelligence is deployed safely, equitably, and without bias.
