Grammar Point: Comparatives & Superlatives & TOEIC Practice

Learn how to compare two or more things, and how to use degree words like much, significantly, and considerably in TOEIC Part 5.

Comparing Things & Degree Words B1–B2 Grammar Focus
Comparison Rules
Comparative: [much / considerably] + [more + adj / -er] + than | Superlative: the + [most + adj / -est]

Core Rules: Follow these rules when making comparisons:

  • 1. Comparing Two Things (Comparative): Compare two items using -er / more ... than (e.g., faster than, more efficient than).
  • 2. Degree Words (How much bigger or smaller): Use words like much, considerably, far, significantly, slightly before comparatives (e.g., considerably more costly, significantly higher).
  • 3. The Top of a Group (Superlative): Use the + -est / the most + adjective for three or more things (e.g., the most viable solution, the cheapest option).
⚠️ TOEIC Trap Alert: Never put very in front of a comparative (NOT very more expensive ❌)! When you see than after the blank, you must use a comparative (more costly), never a superlative (most costly).
Propulsion & Energy Examples
✓ "Hydrogen yields significantly higher gravimetric energy density than battery cells." (Comparative)
✓ "Hybrid turboprops represent the most viable interim solution for regional carriers." (Superlative)
TOEIC Diagnostic Practice Part 5: Incomplete Sentences
Sample Item • Reading Section Timed Pace: ~30s

Although sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) is considerably ________ to produce than conventional kerosene, it offers the most immediate route to reducing commercial flight emissions.

(A) costly
(B) more costly
(C) costliest
(D) most costly
💡 Click to Reveal Answer & TOEIC Strategy Analysis ▼
Correct Answer: (B) more costly

Why (B) is correct: The sentence uses the word "than" (than conventional kerosene). When comparing two things with "than", you must use a comparative adjective: more costly. Also, degree words like considerably go in front of comparatives (considerably more costly).

Why the Other Choices Are Incorrect:
  • (A) costly: Simple adjective; cannot be used when followed by than.
  • (C) costliest: Superlative (-est); cannot be followed by than.
  • (D) most costly: Superlative (most); used for comparing 3 or more things with the, never followed by than.
📌 Quick Test Tip: Look for "than" in the sentence. If you see "than", immediately eliminate regular adjectives and superlatives (most / -est). Choose the comparative (more / -er)!
