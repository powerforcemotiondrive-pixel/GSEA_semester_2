Grammar Point: Prepositions of Place, Direction & Movement

Master spatial and directional prepositions across passenger concourses, airside aprons, and runway movement areas for high-frequency TOEIC Part 5 questions.

Airport Preposition Architecture B1–B2 Grammar Focus
Spatial Matrix
AT (Point/Gate) • IN (Enclosed Room) • ON (Surface/Apron) • ACROSS / ALONG (Movement)

Static Positions vs. Dynamic Direction:

  • 1. AT (Specific Service Points & Nodes): Counters, desks, and intersections (*"Check in at the automated kiosk"* / *"Hold at Holding Point Alpha"* / *"Board at Gate B22"*).
  • 2. IN (Enclosed Volumes & Concourse Halls): Inside rooms or buildings (*"Wait in the departure lounge"* / *"Shop in duty-free"* / *"Inspect engines in Hangar 2"*).
  • 3. ON (Pavement Surfaces & Movement Lines): Flat operational ground (*"Parked on the apron"* / *"Taxiing on Taxiway Bravo"* / *"Aligning on Runway 09L"* / *"Centered on the yellow line"*).
  • 4. Directional Prepositions (Through / Along / Across): Path and transit (*"Walk through security screening"* / *"Taxi along the centerline"* / *"Never drive across an active runway"*).
⚠️ TOEIC Part 5 Preposition Trap: Beware of verb collocations! Static locations use at, in, on (*wait at the gate*). Motion towards a destination requires to, into, onto, across (*proceed to the gate*, *walk onto the jet bridge*). Don't confuse stationary states with directional transit!
Airfield & Concourse Examples
✓ "Passengers must pass through security before entering the retail concourse."
✓ "Flight 840 is cleared to taxi along Taxiway Juliet and hold short at Runway 27."
TOEIC Diagnostic Practice Part 5: Incomplete Sentences
Sample Item • Reading Section Timed Pace: ~30s

Ground vehicles servicing aircraft on the terminal apron are strictly forbidden from driving ________ the marked runway boundary lines without obtaining direct clearance from air traffic control.

(A) across
(B) into
(C) along
(D) between
Click to Reveal Correct Answer & Explanations
CORRECT ANSWER (A) across

Grammar Analysis: The preposition "across" denotes transverse motion from one side of a surface boundary to the other (*driving across a line or runway*). In TOEIC Part 5, spatial prepositions of traversal (across) are frequently tested against parallel motion (along) or interior motion (into).

Why the Other Choices Are Incorrect:
  • (B) into: Implies entering an enclosed container or volume, not crossing a flat two-dimensional ground boundary line.
  • (C) along: Describes motion parallel to a line (e.g., *driving along the road*), which contradicts the warning against penetrating a runway boundary.
  • (D) between: Requires two separate reference entities (e.g., *between Gate 4 and Gate 5*).
📌 TOEIC Strategy Rule: Always check whether the verb denotes traversal (crossing over: across), linear alignment (parallel to: along), or entry into an enclosed space (into / through).
